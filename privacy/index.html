1. Who we are and what this policy covers

Speedrun AI Labs is an Australian artificial intelligence lab based in Sydney, New South Wales. We build, deploy and support two AI agents for businesses: Anton, a cyber security agent, and Joseph, a compliance agent for financial-services regulated businesses (together, the Agents). We also publish practitioner research papers and free open-source skills.

This policy covers personal information we handle in connection with:

  • our website at speedrunlab.ai and www.speedrunlab.ai (the Website), including the contact form;
  • enquiries, sales conversations and our relationships with the businesses we work with and the people who represent them;
  • the installation, deployment, configuration, integration, staff training, support and maintenance of the Agents for businesses (the Services), which we supply under a separate written agreement with each business (the Services Agreement);
  • our dealings with suppliers, contractors, advisers and, if applicable, people who apply to work with us; and
  • our business operations generally.

Personal information has the meaning given in the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) (the Privacy Act): information or an opinion about an identified individual, or an individual who is reasonably identifiable, whether or not it is true and whether or not it is recorded in a material form. Sensitive information is a subset of personal information that includes information about a person’s health, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, sexual orientation, criminal record and biometric information.

The Privacy Act and the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) in Schedule 1 to it regulate how many Australian organisations handle personal information. The Privacy Act does not apply to some small businesses. Whether or not it applies to us at a given time, we have chosen to handle personal information in accordance with the APPs as a matter of good practice, and this policy is written to meet the requirements that the APPs set for a privacy policy. Where the Privacy Act applies to us, we comply with it as a matter of law. We also comply with other Australian laws that govern personal information in our business, including the Spam Act 2003 (Cth) and the privacy-related provisions of the Telecommunications Act 1997 (Cth) and the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth).

This policy does not cover the privacy practices of the businesses that deploy our Agents, or of any third party, including the third-party AI model providers and platforms described in sections 10 and 11. Their own privacy policies govern how they handle personal information.

2. How our agents are deployed, and who is responsible for the data

This section describes the most important thing to understand about our Agents and personal information. It applies to every deployment of Anton or Joseph and is reflected in each Services Agreement.

2.1 Our role: we build, deploy and support the tool

We design and build the Agents, install or deploy them for a business, configure them to that business’s context, integrate them with the systems the business chooses, train the business’s staff and provide ongoing support and maintenance. The Agents are productivity tools. They are not licensed professionals and they do not replace the judgement of the people who use them.

2.2 The deploying business decides what the agent does with data, and is responsible for it

Each business that deploys an Agent (a Deploying Business) decides what data the Agent can access, how that data is collected, used, stored, retained and disclosed, and who within the business may use the Agent and its outputs. The Deploying Business is responsible for the data its Agent accesses, generates and processes (Business Data), including any personal information in it. In particular, the Deploying Business is responsible for:

  • its own privacy policy, collection notices and the consents it obtains from its customers, staff and other people whose personal information its Agent may handle;
  • having a lawful basis for the Agent to collect, use and disclose personal information in the way the business has configured it;
  • its obligations under the Privacy Act and the APPs where they apply to it, including the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme in relation to Business Data;
  • the rules of its sector and its licences, including (for financial-services businesses) obligations under its Australian financial services licence or Australian credit licence, its obligations to the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC), the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) and the Australian Transaction Reports and Analysis Centre (AUSTRAC), and any outsourcing, operational-risk or information-security standards that apply to it;
  • its choice of hardware, hosting environment, integrations, data sources and third-party AI model providers, and its acceptance of their terms;
  • the security of its own premises, networks, devices and systems, and the access controls it places around the Agent;
  • deciding how the Agent’s outputs are used in its decisions, and ensuring that a suitably qualified person reviews outputs before they are acted on; and
  • telling its own people, in the way its own law and policies require, that it uses an AI agent and what that means for them.

2.3 We act only on the deploying business’s instructions

In relation to Business Data, we act only as a service provider acting on the instructions of the Deploying Business, as set out in the Services Agreement. We do not decide what Business Data is collected, why it is collected or what is done with it, and we do not use Business Data for our own purposes. Where an Agent runs on the Deploying Business’s own hardware or in its own environment, we do not store, transmit or have ongoing access to Business Data outside active support sessions, which are carried out with the Deploying Business’s permission as described in section 10. Where the Services Agreement provides for us to host or operate any part of a deployment, it sets out what we hold, where it is held, and the protections and access rules that apply. Where this policy and a Services Agreement deal with the same subject, the Services Agreement prevails as between us and that Deploying Business.

2.4 What we are responsible for

We are responsible for personal information we collect and hold for our own purposes: to run the Website, to respond to enquiries, to enter into and perform Services Agreements, to manage our relationships with the people who represent the businesses we work with, to deal with suppliers and advisers, and to run our business. The rest of this policy describes how we handle that information.

2.5 A note on the agents themselves

Each Agent is deployed and configured for a single Deploying Business. Anton is recommended to run on the Deploying Business’s own hardware on its premises where the business can accommodate that; it can also be set up in another way to suit the business, and depending on the configuration and the third-party services the business chooses, some data may leave the premises. Joseph can be deployed in a cloud environment or entirely inside the Deploying Business’s own environment, for the whole business or for a department. The deployment and hosting arrangement for each Agent, including whether any part of it is hosted or operated by us, is not fixed in advance: it depends on the Services Agreement and the discussion with each client, and the Services Agreement records the arrangement. In every case the Deploying Business decides what data the Agent can access and is responsible for it, and the recorded arrangement determines where Business Data is held and which third parties (if any) process it.

3. The kinds of personal information we collect

We collect only the personal information that is reasonably necessary for our functions and activities. The kinds of personal information we collect depend on how you deal with us.

When you visit the Website, our hosting provider’s servers automatically record technical information about each request, including your IP address, the type and version of your browser and operating system (your user agent), the pages and files requested, the date and time of the request, and the referring page if your browser sends one. When you send us a message through the contact form, we collect your name, your email address, the subject of your message if you give one, your telephone number if you give one, the content of your message and the date and time you submitted it (recorded in Sydney time). If you email us directly, we receive your email address, anything you include in the message and the technical information attached to the email.

3.3 Enquirers and prospective customers

If you enquire about our Agents on behalf of a business, we may collect your name, job title, employer, work contact details, the nature of your enquiry, information about your business’s needs, systems and regulatory context that you choose to share with us, notes of our conversations and meetings, and the history of our dealings with you.

Where a business asks us to configure an Agent to send reports to named people, we hold those people’s names and work contact details as part of the configuration.

3.5 Support sessions

During a support session we may see personal information held in the Deploying Business’s systems. We do not collect or retain that information for our own purposes. Our record of a support session describes the work we did, not the content of the data we saw. See section 10.

3.6 Suppliers, contractors and advisers

We collect the names, contact details, bank account details, ABNs and other business information of the people and organisations that supply goods or services to us, and the content of our communications with them.

3.7 Job applicants

If you apply to work with us, we collect the information in your application and any supporting documents, including your name, contact details, work history, qualifications, referees and the results of any interviews or assessments. We may collect information about you from your referees and from publicly available professional sources, and we may be required to verify your right to work in Australia.

3.8 Social media, GitHub and other platforms

If you interact with us on LinkedIn or GitHub, or on another platform we use, we may collect your public profile information and the content of your interaction. Those platforms handle your information under their own privacy policies, which we do not control.

3.9 Sensitive information

We do not ask for sensitive information and we do not need it to deal with you. If you choose to include sensitive information in a message to us, or we see it in the course of a support session, we handle it in accordance with this policy and the APPs, we use it only for the purpose for which it was given, and we will not collect or keep it unless that is necessary. 9. Cookies, logs and similar technologies

No advertising or analytics cookies. The Website does not use advertising, tracking or analytics cookies, and we do not use third-party trackers or social media pixels. We do not need to show you a cookie banner because we do not set cookies for those purposes.

Self-hosted fonts. The typefaces on the Website are served from our own domain, so your browser does not request them from a third-party font service.

Cloudflare Turnstile. When you open the contact form, Cloudflare Turnstile runs in your browser to tell people from bots without a puzzle. Server logs. Our hosting provider records technical information about each request to the Website, as described in section 3.1, in logs that it keeps for a limited period for security, performance and troubleshooting. Our contact form handler writes short log lines that record whether a submission was accepted or rejected, the reason for a rejection, and a masked form of the sender’s email address. We use these logs to keep the Website and the contact form working and secure, and to investigate abuse.

Links to other sites. The Website links to third-party sites, including GitHub, SSRN, LinkedIn and news publishers. Those sites may set their own cookies and collect information under their own policies when you visit them.

10. Agent data: what we do and do not do

This section describes in more detail how we handle Business Data when we supply the Services. It supplements section 2 and is subject to the Services Agreement with each Deploying Business.

10.1 Where the data lives

Business Data lives in the environment recorded in the Services Agreement: the Deploying Business’s own hardware on its premises, a cloud environment, or another configuration agreed with the business. Whether any part of a deployment is hosted or operated by us depends on the Services Agreement and the discussion with each client. Where an Agent runs on the Deploying Business’s own hardware or inside its own environment, Business Data stays under the Deploying Business’s control and we do not hold a copy. Where the Services Agreement provides for us to host or operate any part of a deployment, the Services Agreement sets out what we hold, where it is held, who may access it, and the protections and access rules that apply; the Deploying Business still decides what data the Agent can access and remains responsible for it.

10.2 Our access to Business Data

Where an Agent runs on the Deploying Business’s own hardware or in its own environment, we do not store, transmit or have ongoing access to Business Data, and our access is limited to active support sessions and to any specific access the Services Agreement describes. Where the Services Agreement provides for us to host or operate any part of a deployment, our access is limited to what the Services Agreement sets out. If a particular deployment requires us to receive technical operating information from the Agent in order to maintain it (for example, version or error information), the Services Agreement will say what is sent and why, and that information will not include Business Data.

10.3 Support sessions

Where an Agent runs in the Deploying Business’s environment, we provide support through secure remote access or supervised sessions, at the Deploying Business’s request and with its permission. The Deploying Business controls when a session starts and ends and what we can see. We ask Deploying Businesses to issue us named, least-privilege accounts for support rather than sharing a staff member’s personal credentials, and to revoke access when it is no longer needed. During a session we may see personal information in the business’s systems. We do not copy, extract or retain it, and our record of the session describes the work done rather than the content we saw. We keep the record for the period described in section 13.

10.4 No training on your data

We do not use Business Data, or any other client data, to train, fine-tune or improve any AI model. Our methodology improves through our own work and research, not through the data of the businesses we serve.

10.5 What the agent does with data is set by the deploying business

What an Agent reads, writes, stores and sends is determined by how the Deploying Business has asked us to configure it and by the integrations the Deploying Business has chosen. For example, Anton reviews the code and infrastructure the Deploying Business points it at and reports to the people the business names; Joseph monitors the obligations and sources the Deploying Business selects and reports changes to the people accountable within the business. In each case the Deploying Business decides the scope, the data sources, the recipients of reports and the retention of the Agent’s outputs.

10.6 Outputs belong to the deploying business

The outputs an Agent generates for a Deploying Business belong to that business. Outputs can be incorrect or incomplete, and their quality depends on the quality, completeness and currency of the data, systems and instructions the Deploying Business gives the Agent. Outputs must be reviewed by a suitably qualified person before they are acted on. The Deploying Business decides how outputs are used and whether they are retained.

10.7 When a services agreement ends

When a Services Agreement ends, the Deploying Business keeps its hardware and its Business Data. We deactivate our proprietary skills and configurations, revoke any remote access we hold, delete any credentials the business issued to us and, where we have hosted or operated any part of the deployment, return or delete the Business Data we hold as the Services Agreement provides. We retain only the records of the engagement we need for our own legal, accounting and record-keeping purposes, as described in section 13.

10.8 What we ask of deploying businesses

Because the Deploying Business controls Business Data, we rely on it to configure the Agent lawfully, to give the Agent access only to the data it needs, to tell its own people what it is doing with an AI agent, to obtain any consents its law requires, to secure its environment, and to review outputs before acting on them. We also rely on it to assess, before deployment, whether an Agent and the chosen deployment model are suitable for the sensitivity of the data involved. Highly sensitive data may call for a deployment that keeps everything inside the Deploying Business’s own environment, and we will say so during scoping. Where Business Data lives on the Deploying Business’s hardware or in its environment, backing up and recovering it are the Deploying Business’s responsibility; where we host or operate any part of a deployment, the Services Agreement sets out who is responsible for backups. We provide guidance on these matters during deployment and training. The Deploying Business remains responsible for its own decisions.

11. Third-party AI model providers and other platforms

Our Agents rely on open-source components and on third-party platforms, large-language-model providers and application programming interfaces (APIs). The Deploying Business chooses the providers its Agent uses, and in most deployments the Agent accesses those providers through the Deploying Business’s own API keys or accounts. That means the Deploying Business has a direct commercial relationship with each provider and is bound by that provider’s terms, privacy policy, data-processing terms and acceptable use policy. Those terms govern how the provider handles any Business Data the Agent sends to it, including whether the provider retains it, where it processes it and whether it uses it to improve its own services. Some providers offer settings or agreements that limit retention or exclude training, and we recommend that Deploying Businesses use them where available. Where a deployment is configured to use models hosted inside the Deploying Business’s own environment, the Agent does not send Business Data to an external model provider; the Services Agreement records which configuration applies.

We do not control those providers, we are not a party to the Deploying Business’s agreements with them, and we do not receive Business Data from them. The Deploying Business is responsible for choosing providers that are suitable for the data involved and for its regulatory obligations, and for monitoring changes to their terms. Where an Agent integrates with other systems the Deploying Business uses, such as customer relationship, accounting, email, calendar, messaging or file-storage systems, those systems are the Deploying Business’s own, and the data stays within the Deploying Business’s arrangements with those vendors.

12. How we protect personal information

We take reasonable steps to protect the personal information we hold from misuse, interference and loss, and from unauthorised access, modification and disclosure. Those steps are proportionate to the sensitivity of the information and the harm that could result, and they include:

  • using reputable providers for hosting, email and business tools, and configuring them securely;
  • serving the Website over HTTPS with security headers, and running the contact form through server-side checks that reject invalid, automated and abusive submissions before any message is sent;
  • keeping the personal information we hold to a minimum, including masking email addresses in our own logs;
  • controlling access to our systems and accounts, including multi-factor authentication where available, and limiting access to the people who need it;
  • using secure, permissioned remote access for support, and never retaining Business Data from support sessions;
  • keeping software up to date and applying the security practices we recommend to the businesses we work with; and
  • training the people who work with us on their privacy and security obligations.

No method of transmission over the internet or of electronic storage is completely secure. We cannot guarantee the security of information you send to us over the internet, and you do so at your own risk. Please do not send us passwords, access keys, card numbers or other secrets through the contact form or by email; if we need to exchange credentials with a Deploying Business we will agree a secure method.

13. Retention and destruction

We keep personal information only for as long as it is needed for the purposes for which it was collected, for a related purpose, or as required by law, and then we take reasonable steps to destroy it or to de-identify it. As a guide:

  • Contact form messages and enquiries are kept while we respond to and follow up your enquiry and for a reasonable period afterwards in case you contact us again or the enquiry leads to an engagement. If it does not, we delete or de-identify the record once it is no longer needed.
  • Engagement records, including Services Agreements, correspondence, invoices, payment records and records of training and support, are kept for the life of the engagement and for at least seven years afterwards, because Australian corporations and tax laws require us to keep financial and business records for that period.
  • Access credentials a Deploying Business issues to us are deleted when the engagement or the relevant support arrangement ends, and sooner on request.
  • Website server logs are kept by our hosting provider for a limited period that it sets, and our contact form handler’s log lines are kept for a similarly short period.
  • Job applications are kept for the recruitment process and for a reasonable period afterwards, so that we can consider you for later roles if you agree and respond to any questions about the process, and are then deleted.
  • Marketing contact details are kept until you opt out or the information becomes stale, after which we delete or suppress them.

We may keep information for longer where we reasonably need it to resolve a dispute, to comply with a legal obligation, to respond to a regulator or because it is part of a record we are required to keep. When we destroy information, we do so securely.

14. Data breaches

14.1 Our own data

If personal information we hold is lost, or accessed or disclosed without authorisation, we will act promptly to contain the incident, assess it and reduce the risk of harm. Where the Privacy Act applies to us and the incident is an eligible data breach under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme in Part IIIC of the Privacy Act (that is, a reasonable person would conclude that the incident is likely to result in serious harm to one or more individuals and we have not been able to prevent that risk with remedial action), we will notify the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) and the affected individuals as the scheme requires. Whether or not the scheme applies to us, if we believe an incident is likely to cause you serious harm we will tell you what happened, what information was involved and what you can do about it.

14.2 Business Data

The Deploying Business controls Business Data, and it is the Deploying Business that must assess an incident affecting Business Data and decide whether, how and to whom to notify under the Privacy Act, its sector rules and its own policies. If we become aware of a security incident involving Business Data that arises from our systems, our access or our conduct, we will notify the Deploying Business without undue delay, give it the information we have, and cooperate with its assessment, containment and notification. Where both we and a Deploying Business hold the same personal information and an eligible data breach occurs, the Privacy Act allows one of us to notify on behalf of both; the Services Agreement describes how we will coordinate.

15. Access and correction

You may ask us for access to the personal information we hold about you, and you may ask us to correct it if it is inaccurate, out of date, incomplete, irrelevant or misleading. To make a request, email info@speedrunlab.ai. We will need to verify your identity before we give access or make a correction, and we may ask you to clarify what you are looking for.

We will respond within a reasonable period, and in any event within 30 days. We do not charge for making a request. If giving access would involve significant work, we may charge a reasonable amount that reflects our costs, and we will tell you before we incur them. There are some situations in which the Privacy Act allows or requires us to refuse access or correction, for example where giving access would unreasonably affect someone else’s privacy, would prejudice legal proceedings or would be unlawful. If we refuse, we will tell you why in writing, unless it would be unreasonable to do so, and we will tell you how you can complain. If we refuse to correct information, you may ask us to attach a statement to it saying that you believe it is inaccurate, out of date, incomplete, irrelevant or misleading, and we will take reasonable steps to do so.

If your request concerns personal information that an Agent handles for a Deploying Business, we will direct you to that business, because it controls the information and is responsible for responding to requests about it. Where the Deploying Business asks for our help to respond to your request, we will provide it under the Services Agreement.
