3. AI and agent disclaimer

Anton and Joseph are AI agents built on large language models and other software, configured by Speedrun AI Labs to the context of each business that deploys them. This disclaimer applies to both agents, to every description, demonstration and example on this website, and to every deployment, subject to the Services Agreement for that deployment.

3.1 What the agents are

The agents are productivity tools. They support the judgement of a business’s security, compliance and leadership teams; they do not replace it. They are not licensed professionals, they are not a law practice, a financial adviser, an auditor, a compliance consultant or a penetration-testing firm, and they do not owe anyone a professional duty of care. They operate within the scope, data sources and permissions the deploying business gives them, and their outputs depend on the quality, completeness and currency of the data, systems and instructions they receive.

3.2 Anton (cyber security)

Anton reviews a business’s code and infrastructure for weaknesses and reports in plain English to the people the business names. A human stays in the loop: the business’s own people decide what is fixed, and when. Anton does not guarantee that it will detect every vulnerability, misconfiguration or weakness, and it cannot prevent every attack. Security is a property of a whole organisation, its people, processes and systems, and no tool can make a business secure on its own. Anton’s findings may include false positives (reported issues that are not real) and false negatives (real issues that are not reported), and a clean report is not a certification, an assurance or a guarantee that systems are free of vulnerabilities. Anton is not a substitute for independent penetration testing, security audits or certifications where a law, regulator, standard, customer or insurer requires them. Anton must be used only on systems that the deploying business owns or operates, or is expressly authorised in writing to test (see section 4).

3.3 Joseph (compliance)

Joseph keeps watch on the regulatory obligations that a financial-services regulated business selects, and reports what has changed to the people accountable within the business. Joseph does not provide legal, financial, tax or compliance advice, and nothing it produces is advice. Joseph does not interpret the law for a business, does not decide whether a business is compliant, and does not replace the business’s compliance function, its responsible managers, its legal advisers or its auditors. Using Joseph does not by itself make a business compliant with any law, licence condition, regulatory guide, prudential standard, reporting obligation or industry code, and it does not discharge or transfer any obligation the business owes to ASIC, APRA, AUSTRAC, the ATO, the OAIC or any other regulator. Joseph may miss, misread, mis-time or misdescribe a change, and the sources it monitors may be incomplete, delayed or revised. The business remains responsible for identifying the obligations that apply to it, for deciding how to meet them, and for every regulatory decision and lodgement.

3.4 Human review

Every output of either agent must be reviewed by a suitably qualified person before it is relied on or acted on. Outputs can be incorrect, incomplete, out of date, inconsistent or misleading, including when they appear confident and well-reasoned. Over-reliance on an agent without human review is a known risk of AI systems and is a misuse of the agents. The deploying business decides how outputs are used in its decisions and is responsible for those decisions.

3.5 Known risks

Known risks of AI agents include: incorrect, fabricated or incomplete outputs (sometimes called hallucinations); outputs that reflect errors or gaps in the data they were given; changes, outages, rate limits or policy changes by third-party model providers and platforms that affect functionality; misuse of an agent for a purpose it was not designed or configured for; over-reliance without human review; and loss of data held on hardware or in environments outside our control. The Services Agreement describes how these risks are allocated between us and each deploying business. In outline, and subject always to the Australian Consumer Law and the Services Agreement, we are responsible for performing our services with due care and skill, and we are not responsible for loss caused by the deploying business’s own acts or omissions, by its configuration choices made against our guidance, by failures of its hardware or environment, by third-party services, or by acting on an output without the review this disclaimer requires.

5. Responsible disclosure and vulnerability reporting

We build a security agent, and we take the security of our own website seriously. If you believe you have found a security vulnerability in speedrunlab.ai, we want to hear from you, and we will work with you in good faith. This policy explains how to report, what we ask of you, and what you can expect from us.

5.1 Scope

This policy covers the website at www.speedrunlab.ai and speedrunlab.ai, including its pages, static assets and the contact form handler at the /api/contact endpoint, and the open-source skill repositories we publish under the Speedrunlab organisation on GitHub (for the code we author). It does not cover: the infrastructure or services of third parties on which the website relies, including Vercel, Cloudflare, Resend and GitHub, which have their own disclosure programs; SSRN, LinkedIn or any other third-party site we link to; or the agents we deploy for businesses, which run in the environment agreed with each business under its Services Agreement and are outside this policy; a business that wishes to test its own deployment should raise it with us under its Services Agreement. If you find an issue in a third-party service, please report it to that provider.

5.2 How to report

Email info@speedrunlab.ai with the words Security Report in the subject line. Please include: a description of the issue and its potential impact; the URL, endpoint or repository affected; step-by-step instructions to reproduce it; any proof-of-concept code or screenshots, kept to the minimum needed to demonstrate the issue; and how you would like to be credited, if at all. You may report anonymously or under a pseudonym, but we will not be able to keep you informed unless you give us a way to contact you. Please do not send a report through the contact form, which is designed for enquiries and is not a secure channel for technical detail beyond a short note asking us to get in touch.

5.3 Our commitments

We will acknowledge your report within five business days (Sydney). We will investigate, keep you informed of our progress at reasonable intervals, and tell you when the issue is fixed. We aim to fix confirmed vulnerabilities within 90 days of your report, or sooner for serious issues, and we will tell you if we need longer and why. With your agreement, we will credit you publicly when the issue is resolved. We do not currently run a paid bug-bounty program and we do not promise rewards, although we may offer a token of thanks at our discretion.

5.4 Safe harbour

If you carry out security research on the systems within the scope of this policy in good faith and in accordance with it, we consider that research authorised for the purposes of our Terms of Use, we will not initiate or support legal action against you, and we will not make a complaint to police or other authorities about it, in each case to the extent the conduct complies with this policy. We regard research that complies with this policy as access that we have authorised for the purposes of the computer-offence provisions of the Criminal Code Act 1995 (Cth) and the Crimes Act 1900 (NSW), so far as those provisions concern our systems. We cannot authorise access to systems that are not ours, including those of our third-party providers, and we cannot bind third parties or law enforcement; if a third party takes action against you for research that complied with this policy, we will, on request, confirm that your conduct was authorised by us.

5.5 What we ask of you

  • Act in good faith to avoid harm: do not degrade, disrupt or deny service to the website or the contact form, and do not run automated scanning at volumes that could do so.
  • Do not access, modify, delete or exfiltrate data that is not yours. If you encounter personal information or other people’s data, stop, do not save or share it, and tell us.
  • Do not send test submissions through the contact form in bulk, and do not use the form to deliver payloads to our inbox. One or two clearly marked test messages to confirm a finding are acceptable.
  • Do not use social engineering, phishing, physical intrusion or attacks on our people, our premises or our personal accounts.
  • Do not test third-party services, even where the website relies on them.
  • Do not publicly disclose the issue, or share it with anyone else, until we have fixed it and agreed a disclosure date with you, or 90 days have passed since your report, whichever is earlier, unless we agree otherwise. We support coordinated disclosure and will not ask you to stay silent indefinitely.
  • Do not demand payment or threaten disclosure as a condition of reporting. That is not security research.
  • Comply with all laws that apply to you.

5.6 Out of scope findings

The following are generally not security vulnerabilities for the purposes of this policy, although we are happy to hear about them: missing security headers that do not lead to an exploitable condition; the presence or absence of DNS records such as SPF, DKIM or DMARC on domains we do not control; clickjacking on pages with no sensitive actions; reports from automated tools without a demonstrated impact; rate-limiting of the contact form; and version disclosure without an exploitable vulnerability.
